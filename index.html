Screens for quadriceps or patellar tendon rupture, patellar fracture and quadriceps inhibition.

How to perform

  1. Supine, knee fully extended. Ask the patient to lift the straight leg 20–30 cm and hold.
  2. Watch the knee: does it stay locked in full extension?
  3. If it bends, measure the lag: the gap between passive and active extension (°).
  4. Palpate above and below the patella for a defect; compare patellar height with the other side.

Positive / result

Unable to lift the straight leg or hold full extension, with a palpable gap or abnormal patellar height → extensor mechanism rupture until proven otherwise.

Interpretation

Lag with an intact mechanism (post-op, effusion) = quadriceps inhibition → NMES, cryotherapy, effusion control.

Accuracy & evidence Evidence: Expert opinion

Dossier page 1 · Triage & red flags

Grades knee effusion reliably; the grade drives how much load the knee can take.

Brush / Stroke / Bulge / Swipe Test⎟Knee Swelling · Open on YouTube

How to perform

  1. Supine, knee extended and relaxed.
  2. Stroke upward along the medial side from joint line to suprapatellar pouch 2–3 times.
  3. Stroke downward along the lateral side and watch the medial sulcus for a returning wave.

Positive / result

0: no wave · Trace: small wave medially · 1+: larger bulge medially · 2+: fluid returns medially without the downstroke · 3+: fluid cannot be moved out of the medial side.

Interpretation

Use the grade to dose activity: rising effusion after a session = too much load.

Accuracy & evidence Evidence: Moderate

Inter-rater κ 0.75; 73% exact agreement (Sturgill 2009).

Sources

  • Sturgill LP et al. Interrater reliability of a clinical scale to assess knee joint effusion. JOSPT 2009;39:845-9

Hip pathology refers to the knee. In adolescents, slipped capital femoral epiphysis (SCFE) is the classic miss.

Key points

  1. Any child or adolescent with knee or thigh pain and a limp: examine the hip.
  2. Passive hip internal rotation in 90° flexion, compared with the other side.
  3. Flex the hip: does the thigh swing into external rotation (Drehmann sign)?
  4. Adults: hip OA often refers to the knee — screen hip ROM and FADIR / FABER.

Positive / result

Adolescent + reduced or painful hip IR / obligatory external rotation on flexion → urgent hip X-ray (AP + frog-leg) and non-weight-bearing.

Accuracy & evidence Evidence: Expert opinion

Dossier page 1 · Triage & red flags

Multiligament injury can mean a dislocation that has already reduced itself. The popliteal artery is at risk.

Key points

  1. Suspect it with gross instability in extension, ≥ 2 ligaments injured, or a high-energy mechanism.
  2. Palpate dorsalis pedis and posterior tibial pulses on both sides; check capillary refill.
  3. Measure ankle systolic pressure (Doppler) and brachial systolic pressure.
  4. ABI = ankle systolic ÷ brachial systolic.
  5. Test the common peroneal nerve (ankle dorsiflexion, dorsum-foot sensation).

Positive / result

ABI < 0.90 → arterial injury likely → immediate CT angiography / vascular surgery. Normal ABI still needs serial review.

Accuracy & evidence Evidence: Moderate

ABI < 0.90: Sn 100% · Sp 100% · NPV 100% in 38 patients (Mills 2004).

Dossier page 1 · Triage & red flags

Decides who needs a knee radiograph after acute knee trauma. A negative rule safely avoids an X-ray.

Key points

  1. Check exclusions first: injury older than 7 days, pregnancy, altered consciousness, paraplegia, multiple or distracting injuries, isolated skin injury.
  2. Palpate the head of the fibula and the patella (note whether patellar tenderness is isolated).
  3. Ask the patient to flex the knee: can they reach 90°?
  4. Ask the patient to take 4 steps (two transfers onto each leg), both immediately after injury and now; limping counts as able.

Positive / result

Any one finding: age ≥ 55 · isolated patellar tenderness · fibular head tenderness · unable to flex to 90° · unable to bear weight for 4 steps → knee radiograph.

Interpretation

Built for 100% sensitivity, so specificity is modest: a positive rule means "X-ray", not "fracture".

Accuracy & evidence Evidence: High

Pooled Sn 98.5% · Sp 48.6% · −LR 0.05 (Bachmann 2004). Children: Sn 100% (Bulloch 2003).

A hot, swollen knee is septic until proven otherwise — joint destruction can start within days.

Key points

  1. Ask about fever, recent injection, arthroscopy, arthroplasty, skin breaks, immunosuppression, diabetes, IV drug use.

Key points

  1. NICE: age ≥ 45 + activity-related joint pain + no morning stiffness or stiffness ≤ 30 min.
  2. ACR clinical criteria: knee pain + ≥ 3 of age > 50, stiffness < 30 min, crepitus, bony tenderness, bony enlargement, no palpable warmth.

Dossier page 3 · Biomechanics

Quantifies strength for limb symmetry and progression; fix the dynamometer with a strap for the quadriceps.

Key points

  1. Quadriceps: seated, knee 60–90°, strap the dynamometer to the table frame; 3 × 5 s maximal efforts, 30 s rest.
  2. Hamstrings: prone or seated at 90°. Hip abductors: side-lying with a strap above the knee.
  3. Record the best of 3 trials in newtons; measure the lever arm (joint centre to dynamometer).
  4. Torque (Nm/kg) = force (N) × lever arm (m) ÷ body mass (kg). LSI = involved ÷ uninvolved × 100.

Interpretation

Hand-held testing without a strap underestimates strong quadriceps (tester strength limits).

Accuracy & evidence Evidence: Moderate

Dossier page 3 · Biomechanics

Loss of full extension is the key early warning after knee injury and surgery.

Key points

  1. Goniometer: axis on the lateral femoral epicondyle, arms to the greater trochanter and lateral malleolus.
  2. Extension: supine with the heel on a bolster; record hyperextension as negative.
  3. Heel-height difference: prone with the legs off the table edge; measure the height difference between heels.
  4. Flexion: supine heel slide.

Interpretation

Any extension deficit compared with the other side is clinically relevant. Heel-height: ≈ 1 cm ≈ 1° of extension loss. Functional flexion: gait ≈ 65°, stairs ≈ 90–100°, squat ≥ 115°.

Accuracy & evidence Evidence: Expert opinion

Goniometric error ≈ 3–5° between sessions.
